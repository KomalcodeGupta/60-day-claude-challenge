The three numbers tell a coherent story:
Footprint Score: 78/100 (Significant)

This is high but not at the ceiling. It reflects breadth more than depth — 15 apps across very different data categories means your exposure surface is wide. You're not doing one risky thing; you're doing many moderately risky things simultaneously, which compounds.
Privacy Score: 34/100 (Fair)

The gap between 78 and 34 is the real story. A footprint of 78 with a privacy score of 34 means you have significant exposure and relatively few active countermeasures in place. If your privacy score were 70+, the high footprint would be less alarming. The combination is what creates risk.
8 Companies Tracking (High Concentration)

This sounds like a lot, but the concentration problem is actually the opposite of what you'd expect. Having 8 companies sounds diverse, but Google alone accounts for 4 of your 15 apps. So it's simultaneously concentrated (one company knows too much) and broad (7 other companies also hold pieces of you).
The core tension the verdict reveals:
Your app choices optimize for convenience — Google Pay, Google Photos, Google Search, YouTube all work better together. But that convenience is exactly what creates the concentration risk. Each service you add to an ecosystem makes the others more powerful, not just additively but multiplicatively.
What the summary text says that the numbers don't:
iMessage is your only genuinely low-risk app in the set. Everything else has meaningful data exposure. That's worth noting — it means there's no "safe zone" in your current stack to anchor from.
The honest bottom line: A footprint of 78 is manageable. A privacy score of 34 is the actual problem to solve. The improvement simulator showed you could realistically reach 60–65 with 5–6 practical changes — which would shift your verdict from Fair to Good without deleting a single app.
